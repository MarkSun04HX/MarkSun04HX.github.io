Bruno Guimarães is through the door. Christos Tzolis is in the building. And yet the window still feels **unfinished** — because Arsenal’s squad shape, not their ambition, is dictating what happens next.

With roughly three weeks left before the deadline, the champions still need clarity in three places: a **left winger** who can start or rotate without collapsing the attack, a **centre-forward** who turns chances into ruthlessness in Europe, and **centre-back / right-back rotation** after **William Saliba**’s long-term World Cup injury exposed how thin the back line can look when minutes stack up. Andrea Berta and Mikel Arteta are not shopping randomly. They are trying to solve a specific geometry problem — and the names in the press right now all map onto it.

Below is a feasibility read on the links dominating the rumour mill: **Bradley Barcola**, **Iliman Ndiaye**, **Kenan Yildiz**, **Jarell Quansah**, **Victor Osimhen**, **Julián Álvarez**, and **Ezri Konsa**.

## What Arsenal still need (and why)

**Left winger:** Leandro Trossard is gone. Gabriel Martinelli is the subject of **Galatasaray** interest and may not finish the window at the Emirates. Tzolis helps, but he is not a like-for-like replacement for every game state — especially when opponents sit deep and Arsenal need a different kind of 1v1 threat on the left.

**Centre-forward:** Missing out on **Vinícius Júnior** forced a reset. The club still wants a forward who can **start knockout games** and share the scoring burden in a way that pure rotation cannot fake forever.

**CB / RB rotation:** Saliba’s absence is the headline, but the underlying issue is broader: **Ben White**’s availability, **Jurriën Timber**’s workload, and the need for a defender who can cover **both centre-back and right-back** without changing the team’s defensive personality.

---

## Jarell Quansah — active talks, sensible profile

**Link status:** **Hot.** Sky Sports reported on **14 August** that Arsenal are in talks with **Bayer Leverkusen** over Quansah — news that broke the same day Liverpool confirmed they will **not** trigger his **£70m** buy-back clause.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Quansah is **23**, England-capped, comfortable in possession, strong in the air, and — crucially — **CB/RB versatile**. He scored **four Bundesliga goals** last season (seven players in the league scored more from set plays), which matters for a team that treats rest defence and set-piece threat as part of the same job description.

**Feasibility: Medium–high, but not cheap.** Leverkusen do not want to sell and would demand **well above the ~£30m** they paid Liverpool last summer. Arsenal’s interest makes tactical sense — especially with Konsa negotiations dragging — but this is not a discount rescue. It is a **premium for a multi-position defender** in a seller’s market. If the fee stays rational, Quansah is one of the cleaner answers to the CB/RB brief. If Leverkusen push into silly money, Arsenal may be buying redundancy rather than balance.

---

## Ezri Konsa — still the No.1 defensive target, still stuck

**Link status:** **Warm, stalled.** Konsa remains Arsenal’s **primary centre-back target**, but Aston Villa’s valuation (reported around **£60m**) and an opening offer described as **barely above half** of that asking price have slowed momentum. He missed Villa’s **UEFA Super Cup** tie with PSG amid the uncertainty, and **Liverpool** are also monitoring.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Premier League proven, England international, physically reliable, and a straightforward upgrade on “hope and youth” when Saliba is out. Konsa is the safe spreadsheet signing.

**Feasibility: Medium–high if Villa blink; low if they don’t.** Villa’s squad looks increasingly resigned to a sale, but Arsenal cannot keep lowballing and expect gratitude. If Quansah talks accelerate, Konsa becomes a **negotiating lever** as much as a first choice — two English defenders, one deal probably gets done. Without movement on fee, this drags into deadline chaos.

---

## Bradley Barcola — talent yes, transaction nightmare

**Link status:** **Persistent, expensive.** Barcola has been on Arsenal’s shortlist all summer. After the Vinícius pursuit ended, PSG winger interest re-emerged — with **Liverpool** also in the frame and Luis Enrique publicly admitting uncertainty over Barcola’s future.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Direct, fast, left-sided, and young enough (**23**) to grow into a title defence. He profiles as the kind of wide forward who can **win a Champions League tie** with one isolated duel.

**Feasibility: Low–medium.** PSG’s reported valuation sits around **£145m** (some outlets cite even higher). Barcola is **not Premier League proven**, and multiple reports suggest he **favours Liverpool** if he leaves Paris. Arsenal’s path here looks like **overpay + gazump** — possible for a club with resources, but terrible value unless the fee collapses. Arteta needs a left-sided attacker; he does not need a trophy-signing regret. Barcola is the flashiest name and the hardest deal.

---

## Iliman Ndiaye — the pragmatic wide alternative

**Link status:** **Monitoring.** Everton’s **Iliman Ndiaye** has been linked for weeks. Arsenal hold interest but, per multiple reports, have **not yet made a formal approach**. **Al-Hilal** are also pushing.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Ndiaye is **26**, Premier League tested, and one of the league’s most dangerous dribblers — only **Jeremy Doku** completed more dribbles last season by some counts. He offers **left-sided unpredictability** without the Ligue 1 adaptation tax.

**Feasibility: Medium.** Everton are confident he will **not push for a move** and would want **£100m+** to change their mind. That is still cheaper than Barcola in theory, but not “easy.” If Martinelli exits and Arsenal want a **PL-ready LW** rather than a lottery ticket, Ndiaye is the grown-up option — provided Everton actually sell, which is not guaranteed.

---

## Kenan Yildiz — the creative dream that Juventus keep blocking

**Link status:** **Cold, periodically reheated.** Arsenal enquired about **Kenan Yildiz** early in the window. **Juventus** made it clear the **21-year-old** is **not for sale**, and recent reports suggest any renewed push risks another dead end.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Versatile across the forward line, elite ball carrier, young, and exactly the type of **high-ceiling creator** Arteta prefers in hybrid roles.

**Feasibility: Low.** Juventus remain adamant. Continuing to pursue Yildiz burns time the window does not have. He belongs on the board as a **long-term admire**, not a realistic August solution unless something breaks behind the scenes.

---

## Julián Álvarez — the dream CF Arteta cannot quite land

**Link status:** **Warm admiration, cold seller.** Álvarez is widely described as Arsenal’s **“dream” centre-forward** after the Vinícius blow — the profile Arteta would pick if he had a magic wand. **Atlético Madrid** insist he is **not for sale at any price** this summer; **Barcelona**’s interest has cooled as they look elsewhere, which theoretically opens a lane — but only if Atléti move.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Pressing intelligence, link play, big-game temperament, and a striker who **fits Arsenal’s rhythm** rather than forcing a system rewrite. Berta and Arteta are both reported admirers.

**Feasibility: Low.** This is the best **football fit** on the list and the worst **business fit** unless Atléti’s stance softens after Álvarez meets with **Diego Simeone** (as some reports suggest may happen). Without a seller, dream targets stay dreams.

---

## Victor Osimhen — the swap-door wildcard

**Link status:** **Active conversation, not yet a bid.** The most intriguing twist of mid-August: Arsenal and **Galatasaray** have held talks where **Osimhen’s name surfaced** alongside Galatasaray’s interest in **Gabriel Martinelli** and **Ethan Nwaneri**. The Telegraph, Sky, and talkSPORT have all carried versions of the same story — a **swap or player-plus-cash** structure is on the table in principle.

**Why Arsenal like him:** Osimhen is **27**, proven across **Bundesliga, Ligue 1, Serie A, and the Süper Lig**, and exactly the kind of **physical, ruthless No.9** who could define a Champions League campaign. Galatasaray value him anywhere from **€130m to €150m** in some reports; earlier this month Arsenal’s attempt involving **Viktor Gyökeres plus cash** was rejected.

**Feasibility: Medium — but messy.** A straight cash deal looks prohibitive. A swap only works if Arsenal are genuinely willing to lose **Martinelli or Nwaneri** — and Martinelli’s reported **reluctance** to move to Turkey complicates the cleanest structure. **Tottenham** are also linked, which adds urgency. Osimhen is feasible **as a package deal**, not as a simple invoice. Berta’s seller-first summer suddenly makes more sense: outbound assets may be the currency for the CF upgrade.

## Final thought

Arsenal are not panicking. They are **prioritising** — Guimarães and Tzolis already upgraded two departments. What remains is the hard part: paying the right price for the right **shape**, not the loudest name.

Quansah and Konsa solve the same defensive anxiety from different angles; one probably happens. Up front, the club must choose between **Barcola’s ceiling and price tag**, **Ndiaye’s Premier League practicality**, and **Osimhen’s swap-window gymnastics**, while **Álvarez** and **Yildiz** hover as moves that need a seller to blink first.

Three weeks. Three positions. Seven names. The window is not about volume anymore — it is about **finishing the squad** before the first whistle proves what the table already suspects: champions are judged on depth, not headlines.
