Arsenal travel to the **Metropolitano** on **Wednesday, 29 April 2026** (**8:00 pm UK**) for the **first leg** of the **Champions League semi-final** against **Diego Simeone’s Atlético Madrid**. The return is **six days later** in north London (**Tuesday, 5 May**, same kick-off). After [edging Sporting on aggregate]({% post_url 2026-04-16-arsenal-sporting-champions-league-post-match %}), this is the night that decides whether the tie is **open** or **suffocating** before the Emirates crowd gets its say.

The headline from October is hard to ignore: Arsenal already beat this opponent **4-0** in the **league phase**—**Viktor Gyökeres** twice, plus **Gabriel** and **Gabriel Martinelli**. Treat that as **psychology**, not a **script**. Atlético scrapped through a **play-off** with **Club Brugge**, outlasted **Tottenham** on aggregate, then **eliminated Barcelona** to reach their first semi since **2016/17**; they will not volunteer a repeat of the autumn mismatch.

## Form and mood (broad strokes)

Domestically, Arsenal are still in the **title** conversation but the schedule bites: a **2–1** loss at **Manchester City** (**19 April**) was followed by a **1–0** win over **Newcastle** (**25 April**) that, by some accounts, took a heavy **physical** toll. Atlético, with La Liga no longer a live title race for them, still sent out a **strong** side in a **3–2** win over **Athletic Club** on the weekend—**Alexander Sørloth** scored twice on his return, while **Ademola Lookman** was **not** in the squad, a file worth watching for Wednesday.

## Injury board (what the build-up suggests)

Nothing here is official team news the morning of the match—just the shape of the conversation **days** out.

**Arsenal:** **Mikel Arteta** has referred to **“little niggles”** for **Kai Havertz** and **Eberechi Eze** after recent games, while **Jurriën Timber** and **Riccardo Calafiori** were **not** involved against Newcastle. That cluster forces **honest** uncertainty about who starts in Madrid versus who is **protected** for the run-in.

**Atlético:** **Pablo Barrios** had to come off against Athletic with a problem serious enough that some Spanish reports fear **weeks**, not days—potentially **both** legs of the tie. **Lookman’s** absence from the matchday group at the weekend keeps the **left-sided** threat picture fluid.

**Front three:** Trossard – Gyökeres – Madueke  

**Midfield:** Rice – Zubimendi – Ødegaard  

**Defense:** Kiwior – Gabriel – Saliba – White

If **Havertz** and **Eze** are short of **90-minute** sharpness, I lean **Ødegaard** as the advanced **eight** with **Rice** and **Zubimendi** as the **double pivot**. Upstairs, **Gyökeres** carries the **reference** role Simeone’s centre-backs will want to **bend**; **Madueke** and **Trossard** are the **isolation** merchants against tired legs. At the back, **Kiwior** is the conservative pick if **Timber** / **Calafiori** are not cleared—**duels** in the channel will matter more than **overlap** glamour.

## Atlético’s likely posture

Simeone will not reinvent himself for the narrative. Expect **compact** spacing, **aggressive** rest defence when Arsenal’s full-backs step in, and **set pieces** treated like **open-play** goals. The weekend front four—**Alex Baena**, **Sørloth**, **Antoine Griezmann**, **Giuliano Simeone**—hints at **interchange** and **second-ball** hunting rather than a pure **low-block** museum piece.

Arsenal’s task is **patience with teeth**: enough **risk** to land an **away** goal, not enough **chaos** to feed **one-pass** transitions. The **4-0** in autumn proves Atlético *can* be **cut** open; it does **not** prove they will **volunteer** the same spaces in a **two-legged** semi.

## Game outlook

Three scripts feel live:

1. **Atlético** score first, the crowd **ignites**, and Arsenal spend long stretches **managing** a **rope** rather than hunting a second.  
2. **Arsenal** nick an **away** goal early, Simeone **tweaks** upward pressure, and the game opens into **transition** tennis—where **Saliba** / **Gabriel** duels and **Raya**’s **sweeping** matter as much as chance creation.  
3. A **0-0** or **1-1** that looks different on paper: a **clean sheet** in Madrid is **currency**, but a **score draw** for the visitors can still be **excellent** business.

What you want to avoid is the **in-between**: lots of **possession** between the boxes, few **entries** into the **prime** shooting belt, and a growing sense that **one** set piece could **tilt** the emotional scoreboard.

## Osimhen rumours (window noise)

Links to **Victor Osimhen** remain **speculative**, but the profile maps cleanly onto what big clubs want from a **number nine**: **movement**, **penalty-box** presence, and **finishing** under pressure—the sort of player who can decide a **half-chance** in a **knockout** tie. Whether Arsenal **prioritize** that tier of signing this summer depends on **budget**, **seller** timing, and **competition** from elsewhere in Europe. Until something is **concrete**, it is **paper** talk—and Madrid is still the **only** appointment that matters this week.

## Final thoughts

This leg is **not** about **re-enacting** October. It is about **weathering** the **Metropolitano**, **limiting** **regression** moments, and—if the night allows—banking a **goal** that changes how **May 5** feels under the **Emirates** lights.

Win the **evening’s emotional physics**, and London gets a **semi-final** rather than a **rescue mission**.
